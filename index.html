VII.  road kill

to the deer carcass by the curb, i confess i know your name. the pills i’ve taken may or may not be placebo. there is sugar on the back of my tongue and i know now that flesh left out to dry is sweet as it is sour as it is lonely. the light is gentle and warm, even as it hits cold skin and open mouths, a body next to a body, dead things soft and small, blood staining the intersection, somewhere sunset-west and north of a mother’s call.

VI.  ( , )

i pretend i do not see what my mama is doing as she pulls out a red envelope, embossed with a golden 福 shining beneath our broken kitchen light i do not hear her humming & gathering what must be dollar bills, crumpled, flattened— the envelope, tucked between my fingers the words she says, tie your shoes happy new year (i love you,) i smile & laugh & say thank you as she hugs me i pretend i do not splinter as i bend inwards to reach her (these winters are frostbitten & heavy— i miss when my mama’s war-torn, oil-burnt, ceramic-calloused palm was large enough to embrace mine whole,) my body is a simple aching comma & my mama will always be steady parentheses her arms ( open ) ( wide ) & i’m afraid i can’t remember the last time i had to look up to count her crow’s feet. i’m afraid i won’t know the last time i ever leave home. i’m afraid i’m not as good of an actor as i think i am. i think i’ll open the red envelope tomorrow morning & count how much money is inside i think i’ll call her & perhaps remember to swallow away the fear of things that haven’t happened yet she’ll tell me, laoye would’ve been 100 years old this new year. well, ma, you have to make it to 100. perhaps i won’t tell her. (inside the red envelope, three dog-eared 20 dollar bills.)

V.  a new horizon, always

i never thought i’d see other skies so blue.
an evergreen tree, rotting alone,
there was a time when this was all i knew.

frame of my childhood bed, walls i outgrew
after bleeding & being flown;
i never thought i’d see other skies so blue.

the plane lands, mountains come into view
a valley i know as first-home—
there was a time when this was all i knew.

my mama tracks my location & calls, 去去
i smile as i hang up the phone;
i never thought i’d see other skies so blue.

the streets blur by, only a familiar few
she says, “you look tired & grown”
there was a time when this was all i knew.

hometown friends who stayed, cut through
& say i am half-unknown;
we never thought i’d see other skies so blue
there was a time when this was all i knew.

IV.  seaweed breath

let’s go to the beach
hope it will free us
free me
grab handfuls of sand &
act like the castles in our palms
will stay upright

let’s dive into the sea
just skin
naked as the day we came into this world
fighting for breath
covered in salt & earth &
foam & light &
some other fleshier stuff

the sun hits the horizon
& cradles you
& i can only watch as
your eyes blink
& become ripple

i know i’m stupid
i’ll take the shells you
flung at my face &
bite until tongue meets metal

i’ll strip everything if
it means we get to float away
until our hair grows
into whale bone &
mermaid teeth

Farewell to a Lutist
By Gao Shi

Across a thousand miles, yellow clouds spread, veiling midday sun
The north wind blows, bringing wild geese flying amongst swirling snow.
Grieve not over having no close friends on this road ahead,
For who in the world does not know you?
